# Human–LLM Pair Interoperability Evaluation

## Purpose

This specification evaluates whether a human–model pair can preserve meaning, expose uncertainty, detect error, and retain effective review authority. It does not attempt to infer whether prose was generated by an LLM from style alone.

## Evaluation unit

The evaluation unit is a governed interaction trace containing:

- the initiating human claim or request;
- model responses and revisions;
- vocabulary definitions;
- evidence and assumptions;
- human review actions;
- identified disagreements;
- final published or executed claim;
- attribution and confidence records.

## Capability layers

### Expression capability

The pair can produce a coherent statement. This is necessary but weak evidence.

### Interpretive capability

The human can accurately restate the output, its assumptions, and its intended scope.

### Evaluative capability

The pair can identify contradictions, unsupported claims, vocabulary collisions, evidence gaps, and boundary violations.

No assessment may infer interpretive or evaluative capability solely from expression quality.

## Structural tests

### Meaning preservation

The final claim must remain semantically consistent with the initiating intent, or every transformation must be explicitly disclosed and accepted.

### Vocabulary alignment

Critical terms must have operational definitions. Same-term/different-meaning and different-term/same-structure conflicts must be recorded.

### Boundary control

The pair must state where a claim applies, where it does not apply, and which assumptions limit transfer to other contexts.

### Contradiction detection

Material contradictions within the trace or final artifact must be detected or explicitly marked unresolved.

### Evidence classification

Each support item must be classified as evidence, interpretation, assumption, analogy, speculation, or unknown.

### Audit-gap control

The complexity and consequence of the output must not exceed the reviewer’s demonstrated ability to evaluate it without compensating controls.

### Review comprehension

A human approval event is valid only when the reviewer demonstrates comprehension of the claim, scope, uncertainty, and material objections.

### Revision integrity

When a foundational premise changes, dependent conclusions must be reevaluated rather than cosmetically rewritten to preserve the original outcome.

### Independent reconstruction

A second evaluator should be able to reconstruct the principal reasoning from the recorded premises, definitions, evidence classifications, and transitions.

## Outcomes

- `PASS`: all required structural tests pass and no critical unresolved defect remains.
- `PARTIAL`: the pair preserves the principal meaning but has bounded unresolved defects or insufficient evidence for one or more noncritical tests.
- `FAIL`: a critical test fails, reviewer comprehension is absent, or the final claim materially exceeds its evidence or declared boundary.
- `INDETERMINATE`: required trace information is missing or contradictory enough to prevent a defensible determination.

## Error attribution

An assessment may include multiple attribution classes:

- `human_originated`: a false or unsupported premise entered through the human.
- `model_originated`: the model introduced a false or unsupported claim.
- `interaction_originated`: the sequence produced a defect not attributable to either participant alone.
- `review_failed`: a detectable defect passed through review.
- `translation_originated`: vocabulary substitution or interpretation changed meaning.
- `boundary_originated`: a claim was transferred beyond its valid scope.
- `amplification_originated`: uncertainty was converted into unwarranted confidence or reach.

## Admission rule

A record is structurally admissible for governed publication only when:

1. required identifiers and timestamps are present;
2. all critical tests have determinations;
3. evidence and assumptions are distinguishable;
4. vocabulary collisions are resolved or exposed;
5. reviewer comprehension is demonstrated for high-consequence claims;
6. the overall outcome follows deterministically from test results;
7. unresolved critical defects cause `FAIL` or `INDETERMINATE`.

## Initial scoring guidance

Scores use a 0–1 scale:

- 0.00–0.39: failed
- 0.40–0.69: partial
- 0.70–1.00: passed

A numeric average must never override a critical failure. The deterministic validator applies critical-test precedence before calculating the recommended outcome.
