# Human–LLM Pair Interoperability Evaluation

## Purpose

This specification evaluates whether a human–model pair can preserve meaning, expose uncertainty, detect error, and retain effective review authority. It does not attempt to infer whether prose was generated by an LLM from style alone.

The architecture also recognizes that humans may act as a stateful interoperability layer among otherwise isolated models or entities. That broader mediated-composition model is defined in `docs/GOVERNED_MEDIATED_TRANSITION_COMPOSITION.md`.

## Evaluation unit

The base evaluation unit is a governed interaction trace containing:

- the initiating human claim or request;
- model responses and revisions;
- vocabulary definitions;
- evidence and assumptions;
- human review actions;
- identified disagreements;
- final published or executed claim;
- attribution and confidence records.

For mediated interoperability, the evaluation unit additionally includes source, intermediary, and recipient continuity references; the source distinction; intermediary input and output; recipient reconstruction; resulting transitions; directional rate; permitted scope; fidelity determinations; intentionality states; local admissibility decisions; and null-model controls.

## Capability layers

### Expression capability

The pair can produce a coherent statement. This is necessary but weak evidence.

### Interpretive capability

The human can accurately restate the output, its assumptions, and its intended scope.

### Evaluative capability

The pair can identify contradictions, unsupported claims, vocabulary collisions, evidence gaps, and boundary violations.

No assessment may infer interpretive or evaluative capability solely from expression quality.

## Interoperability levels

The validator and assessment architecture must preserve the following hierarchy:

```text
causal influence
!= information transfer
!= communication
!= interoperability
!= governed composition
```

### Relay

A signal originating from one entity passes through an intermediary and reaches another entity.

### Semantic mediation

A task-relevant distinction survives the intermediary's transformation.

### Adaptive interoperability

One or more participants improve encoding, reconstruction, correction, or routing through repeated interaction.

### Governed composition

The exchange and resulting transitions remain bounded by identity, continuity, authority, consent, policy, evidence, purpose, recoverability, and consequence.

No higher level may be inferred solely because a lower level was observed.

## Structural tests

### Meaning preservation

The final claim must remain semantically consistent with the initiating intent, or every transformation must be explicitly disclosed and accepted.

### Vocabulary alignment

Critical terms must have operational definitions. Same-term/different-meaning and different-term/same-structure conflicts must be recorded.

### Boundary control

The pair must state where a claim applies, where it does not apply, and which assumptions limit transfer to other contexts.

### Contradiction detection

Material contradictions within the trace or final artifact must be detected or explicitly marked unresolved.

### Evidence classification

Each support item must be classified as evidence, interpretation, assumption, analogy, speculation, or unknown.

### Audit-gap control

The complexity and consequence of the output must not exceed the reviewer’s demonstrated ability to evaluate it without compensating controls.

### Review comprehension

A human approval event is valid only when the reviewer demonstrates comprehension of the claim, scope, uncertainty, and material objections.

### Revision integrity

When a foundational premise changes, dependent conclusions must be reevaluated rather than cosmetically rewritten to preserve the original outcome.

### Independent reconstruction

A second evaluator should be able to reconstruct the principal reasoning from the recorded premises, definitions, evidence classifications, and transitions.

## Mediated-composition controls

### Stateful intermediary

The intermediary must be modeled as an adaptive participant whose output depends on current state, context, memory, incentives, authority, and audience. A fixed codec model is insufficient.

### Developing medium

The system must not assume a complete shared language exists before interaction. The medium may change through repeated correspondence and must be versioned when its operational meanings change.

### Intentionality without invention

Communication does not require conscious intent, but each participant's intentionality state should be recorded as `unaware`, `incidental`, `inferred`, or `deliberate`. Intent may not be inferred solely from successful correspondence.

### Directional rate and permitted extent

Rate must be measured separately by direction. Permitted extent must identify which states, vocabulary, capabilities, observations, and authorities may cross each boundary. Rate and scope must not be collapsed into one channel score.

### Multidimensional fidelity

Mediated records must support independent determinations for:

- symbolic fidelity;
- semantic fidelity;
- pragmatic fidelity;
- causal fidelity;
- governance fidelity.

A numeric average may not override a critical fidelity failure.

### Transition-time admissibility

Admissibility is reconstructed for each local transition and may return `allow`, `deny`, `quarantine`, or `defer`. One participant's approval cannot supply another participant's missing standing, authority, consent, evidence, or continuity.

### Identity and continuity

Participant labels are not sufficient continuity evidence. Model versions, sessions, memory state, human operators, intermediary chains, policies, delegations, provenance, and execution environments must be referenced when material.

### Coupling without agency inflation

A coupled system must not be classified as a higher-order agent, shared cognition, or collective identity without additional evidence of persistent joint state, integrated objectives, shared continuity, decision boundaries, joint error correction, and accountable action attribution.

### Intermediary-learning hypothesis

Claims that an outer entity learned a particular intermediary's transformation behavior must distinguish population-level language regularities, individual intermediary patterns, and situational patterns. Tests should include intermediary substitution, context change, paraphrase, delay, adversarial relay, and hidden provenance.

### Null models and falsification

Observed correspondence must be tested against plausible alternatives, including common training, independent convergence, generic language regularities, human-imposed correspondence, prompt contamination, selective reporting, confirmation bias, and coincidence.

## Outcomes

- `PASS`: all required structural tests pass and no critical unresolved defect remains.
- `PARTIAL`: the pair preserves the principal meaning but has bounded unresolved defects or insufficient evidence for one or more noncritical tests.
- `FAIL`: a critical test fails, reviewer comprehension is absent, or the final claim materially exceeds its evidence or declared boundary.
- `INDETERMINATE`: required trace information is missing or contradictory enough to prevent a defensible determination.

For mediated-composition classifications, the outcome and the achieved interoperability level must be reported separately. A passing relay does not constitute adaptive interoperability, and passing adaptive interoperability does not establish collective agency.

## Error attribution

An assessment may include multiple attribution classes:

- `human_originated`: a false or unsupported premise entered through the human.
- `model_originated`: the model introduced a false or unsupported claim.
- `interaction_originated`: the sequence produced a defect not attributable to either participant alone.
- `review_failed`: a detectable defect passed through review.
- `translation_originated`: vocabulary substitution or interpretation changed meaning.
- `boundary_originated`: a claim was transferred beyond its valid scope.
- `amplification_originated`: uncertainty was converted into unwarranted confidence or reach.
- `continuity_originated`: identity or state continuity was assumed without sufficient evidence.
- `mediation_originated`: the intermediary transformed, omitted, reordered, or amplified a distinction.
- `provenance_originated`: source identity or source ordering was lost or misrepresented.
- `agency_inflation`: coupling or correspondence was incorrectly elevated into joint agency or shared cognition.

## Admission rule

A record is structurally admissible for governed publication only when:

1. required identifiers and timestamps are present;
2. all critical tests have determinations;
3. evidence and assumptions are distinguishable;
4. vocabulary collisions are resolved or exposed;
5. reviewer comprehension is demonstrated for high-consequence claims;
6. the overall outcome follows deterministically from test results;
7. unresolved critical defects cause `FAIL` or `INDETERMINATE`;
8. mediated participants have continuity references appropriate to the claim;
9. direction, rate, and permitted scope are not conflated;
10. fidelity dimensions are reported independently;
11. each participant's local transition has an admissibility result;
12. null explanations and controls are declared for claims of adaptive interoperability;
13. unsupported escalation from relay to interoperability or collective agency is rejected.

## Initial scoring guidance

Scores use a 0–1 scale:

- 0.00–0.39: failed
- 0.40–0.69: partial
- 0.70–1.00: passed

A numeric average must never override a critical failure. The deterministic validator applies critical-test precedence before calculating the recommended outcome.
